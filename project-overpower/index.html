CASE STUDY · GAME DESIGN

Project OverPower

A 3v3v3 arena game where three teams fight over one map at the same time, and gold comes from holding ground instead of killing people.

ROLE
Sole designer
ENGINE
Unity, C#, Photon PUN2
SCOPE
Design document and playable prototype
STATUS
In development
WORK IN PROGRESS

The design document is written to grow with the game and the prototype currently covers the core loop and networking. A lot of what is on this page will change once I have more people playing it.

01

The pitch

Project OverPower went from a shower thought about improving MOBAs and team arena brawlers into a full design document and a networked prototype.

Three teams of three enter one triangular map. Each team has a capital to defend and two opponents to pressure at once, so there is never a safe direction to look. Gold comes only from territory you hold, and it buys ability upgrades rather than stat items, so a team that gets ahead gets more options instead of bigger numbers.

When the first team is eliminated the map physically shrinks into two lanes, which cuts the dead time that usually follows a kill in this genre.

02

Core loop

A match runs about 25 minutes. The first three steps build your economy and the last three spend it, which is why the loop is coloured in two halves.

03

Territory economy

Most games in this genre reward you for kills. OverPower rewards you for ground.

Gold comes only from territory, at a fixed rate per second, and you can only capture a zone adjacent to one you already hold. That adjacency rule does most of the work. It turns the map into a supply line rather than a set of separate objectives, and it means a team that wins every fight but holds nothing still loses.

TERRITORY CAPTURE INCOME
Tier 4 · central zone15s10 g/s
Tier 3 · flanking10s10 g/s
Tier 2 · transition15s5 g/s
Tier 1 · capital20snone

Capitals generate nothing. They are where you respawn, heal and buy upgrades, so the safest place on the map is also the least productive. Every purchase costs you time away from the zones paying for it, and that trade is the decision I most want players arguing about.

04

Combat

The second thing separating it from a MOBA is how you fight. There are no auto-attacks and no click-to-target. Every ability is a projectile you aim, so a fight is decided by where you put your cursor and when.

Your abilities run on cooldowns, but primary fire runs on an overheat bar instead. It fills as you shoot, and maxing it out silences you until it drains back to zero, so sustained fire is a resource you spend rather than a rhythm you settle into.

Builds are modular. You pick a starting weapon, then an upgrade that changes what the projectile does, and rockets explode on impact while sawblades pass through people. The final upgrade changes how that behaviour works, not how big its numbers are.

05

The part I am least sure about

The gun kit shows the loop works. The melee system is the one that asks whether the loop can carry something else.

A melee player gives up their equipment ability and buys a weapon instead, with a light and a heavy attack from the start. Upgrading it changes what those two attacks do and lets them be chained into combos specific to that weapon, rather than raising their damage.

This sits outside the current prototype on purpose. I am putting it in the case study because it is the design question I find most interesting: does an economy built around holding ground still work when one team is fighting at a completely different range to everyone else? I do not know yet, and that is the point of building it.

06

Balancing model

I modelled the economy before building it, to answer one question: can a team that falls behind still come back?

TIME GAP BOUNTIES TO CLOSE IT
2 min1120 g2
5 min2800 g4
10 min5600 g7
15 min8400 g10

Ten captures is too many to expect from a team that is already losing, which is the reason capture bounties exist at all. A zone held for five minutes without interruption builds a bounty that pays out to whoever takes it, so the longer a dominant team sits on a lead the more that lead is worth to the people chasing them.

HONEST CAVEAT

This is a model, not a result. I do not have enough playtest data to know whether these numbers survive contact with real players, and I expect several of them to be wrong. It exists so that when the prototype does produce data, I have something to check it against.
